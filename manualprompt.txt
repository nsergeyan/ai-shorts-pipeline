NO-SPOILER RULE (HARD CONSTRAINT — OVERRIDES HYPE)
═══════════════════════════════════
The channel breaks news WITHOUT burning the story. A spoiler kills watch-time, kills comments, and gets the video mass-reported by the fandom. Nothing below is optional.

DEFINE THE SPOILER WINDOW:
- Anything released in the last THIRTY days (episode, film, finale, chapter) is INSIDE the window and is fully spoiler-protected.
- Anything not yet released — leaks, screeners, early reviews, festival cuts, review-embargo breaks, datamined content, ripped clips — is ALWAYS protected regardless of date.
- Anything older than thirty days is outside the window, but the MAJOR-BEAT BAN below still applies to it.

NEVER REVEAL (regardless of age):
- Deaths, resurrections, or a character's final fate
- Twists, betrayals, identity reveals, "he was X all along"
- Endings, finale outcomes, who wins a fight, who survives
- Cliffhangers and post-credit scenes
- Surprise returns, cameos, or casting reveals not shown in official marketing
- Plot points from unreleased or leaked material
- Anything the marketing deliberately hid

ALWAYS SAFE (build the video from these):
- Anything shown in an OFFICIAL trailer, teaser, poster, key art, or press release
- Production facts: how a prop was built, what a set smelled like, a costume constraint, an animation trick, an on-set rule, a filming location quirk
- Worldbuilding rules and lore mechanics that don't decide an outcome
- Character details established well before the current release
- Behind-the-scenes and creator-interview material that discusses craft, not plot resolution
- The mere EXISTENCE of a release ("the new season is out") — that is news, not a spoiler

FRAMING FIX: You may anchor to a fresh episode or film, but the reveal must sit BESIDE the plot, not inside it. Describe the texture, the prop, the sound, the rule, the on-set fact — never the outcome. "With the new season out, nobody noticed what the masks are actually made of" is safe. "With the new season out, here's who dies" is a kill.

THE BLIND-VIEWER TEST (run it on the finished script, out loud):
Imagine someone who has NOT watched the newest release hears this script. If it changes what they expect to happen, tells them a fate, or removes a surprise — REWRITE IT. If they can watch the release afterward with every surprise intact, it passes.

VAGUENESS IS NOT A LOOPHOLE: "Something huge happens to a fan favourite" is still a spoiler. Implying, teasing, or hinting at a protected beat counts as revealing it.

SAFETY BUFFER: If you are UNSURE whether a detail is a spoiler, treat it as one and pick another candidate. There is always another fact.

CAPTION/TITLE RULE: The hook, the on-screen title, and any hashtags must also be spoiler-free. A safe script with a spoiler title still fails.

FRESH-RELEASE SOFTENER (optional, use when anchored to something under seven days old): the script may include one short spoken reassurance early on — for example "no spoilers, I promise" — but this NEVER licenses a spoiler later. It is a trust signal, not a permission slip.

WHAT MAKES A FACT GOOD (READ FIRST)
═══════════════════════════════════
The target is NOT "most obscure." The target is "makes a viewer say wait, WHAT?" — AND can be FRAMED as hidden, dark, or overlooked lore (the channel's signature angle) — AND spoils nothing.

The ideal fact pattern (the "soap pattern"):
A small, concrete, almost ordinary question (a texture, smell, sound, habit, weird detail) applied to a famous or central thing — where the real, canon answer is funnier, darker, or stranger than expected.

This pattern is CAMP-NEUTRAL. In animation it points at lore and production; in live-action it points at props, sets, costumes, stunts, sound design, and on-set rules — the physical reality behind an image everyone has seen. Those are also, conveniently, the most spoiler-safe facts that exist.

THE FRAMING LAYER (this channel's superpower):
Once you have the real detail, frame it the way this channel does — as something hidden, missed, or unsettling: "the disturbing reason," "the detail nobody noticed," "what this scene was really hiding," "the messed-up lore behind."
